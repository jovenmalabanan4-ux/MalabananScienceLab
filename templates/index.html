1. Organ Systems Working Together

The human body has different organ systems that work together to maintain life and keep the body healthy.

Digestive System

  • Breaks down food into nutrients.
  • Mechanical digestion physically breaks food into smaller pieces.
  • Chemical digestion uses enzymes and digestive juices to break down food.
  • Absorption allows nutrients to enter the bloodstream.
  • Elimination removes undigested waste.

Excretory System

  • Removes waste products from the body.
  • The kidneys filter blood and produce urine.
  • The urinary system includes the kidneys, ureters, urinary bladder, and urethra.

Plant Transport System

  • Xylem transports water and minerals from the roots.
  • Phloem transports sugars and food throughout the plant.

2. Heredity

Heredity is the passing of characteristics from parents to their offspring through genes.

Important Terms

  • Genes – Units of heredity that carry genetic information.
  • Dominant Allele – An allele that can be expressed even when only one copy is present.
  • Recessive Allele – An allele expressed when two recessive copies are present.
  • Genotype – The genetic combination of an organism (AA, Aa, or aa).
  • Phenotype – The observable characteristics of an organism.
  • Punnett Square – A diagram used to predict possible genetic combinations in offspring.

Examples of Genotypes

  • AA – Homozygous dominant
  • Aa – Heterozygous
  • aa – Homozygous recessive

3. Taxonomic Classification

Taxonomic classification is the scientific process of grouping organisms based on their similarities and differences.

Six Kingdoms of Life

  1. Archaebacteria – Microorganisms that can survive in extreme environments.
  2. Eubacteria – Common bacteria found in different environments.
  3. Protista – Mostly single-celled organisms, such as amoebas.
  4. Fungi – Organisms such as mushrooms, molds, and yeasts.
  5. Plantae – Organisms that generally produce their own food through photosynthesis.
  6. Animalia – Multicellular organisms that obtain food by consuming other organisms.

Three Domains of Life

  • Bacteria
  • Archaea
  • Eukarya

Taxonomic Hierarchy

  1. Kingdom
  2. Phylum
  3. Class
  4. Order
  5. Family
  6. Genus
  7. Species

4. Photosynthesis, Cellular Respiration, and Natural Cycles

Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis is the process by which plants use sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide to produce glucose and oxygen.

Word Equation
Carbon Dioxide + Water + Light Energy → Glucose + Oxygen

Requirements:

  • Sunlight
  • Water
  • Carbon dioxide
  • Chlorophyll

Cellular Respiration

Cellular respiration is the process by which cells break down glucose to release usable energy called ATP.

Word Equation
Glucose + Oxygen → Carbon Dioxide + Water + Energy (ATP)

Important Organelles

  • Chloroplast – The organelle where photosynthesis takes place.
  • Mitochondria – The organelles where most cellular respiration takes place.

Natural Cycles

Water Cycle: The continuous movement of water through the environment.

  • Evaporation
  • Condensation
  • Precipitation
  • Collection

Carbon Cycle: The movement of carbon between living organisms, the atmosphere, oceans, and Earth's surface.

Oxygen Cycle: The movement of oxygen between living organisms and the environment.

5. Structure of the Atom

An atom is the smallest unit of an element that retains its chemical properties.

Subatomic Particles

  1. Proton – Positively charged particle found in the nucleus.
  2. Neutron – Neutral particle found in the nucleus.
  3. Electron – Negatively charged particle found around the nucleus.

Development of Atomic Models

  1. John Dalton – Proposed that matter is made of tiny particles called atoms.
  2. J.J. Thomson – Proposed the Plum Pudding Model.
  3. Ernest Rutherford – Discovered the small, dense, positively charged nucleus.
  4. Niels Bohr – Proposed that electrons occupy specific energy levels around the nucleus.

Important Atomic Terms

  • Atomic Number – The number of protons in an atom.
  • Mass Number – The total number of protons and neutrons.
  • Electric Charge – A property of particles that can be positive, negative, or neutral.
Formulas
Atomic Number = Number of Protons
Mass Number = Protons + Neutrons
Number of Neutrons = Mass Number − Atomic Number

Quick Review

  1. Organ Systems – How body systems work together.
  2. Heredity – How traits are passed from parents to offspring.
  3. Taxonomy – How living organisms are classified.
  4. Photosynthesis – How plants produce glucose using sunlight.
  5. Cellular Respiration – How cells release energy from glucose.
  6. Natural Cycles – How materials move through nature.
  7. Atomic Structure – The composition and development of atomic models.

Study Tips

  • Understand the definitions of important scientific terms.
  • Memorize the functions of the major organ systems.
  • Practice solving Punnett squares.
  • Learn the six kingdoms and three domains.
  • Understand the processes of photosynthesis and cellular respiration.
  • Memorize the subatomic particles and their charges.
  • Practice drawing and labeling scientific diagrams.
